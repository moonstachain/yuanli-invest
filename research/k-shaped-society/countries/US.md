# US｜1990–2026 K-Shaped Research Dossier

## 1. Scope and data boundary

This dossier is a **desktop research synthesis**, not a vintage-safe 1990–2026 replay. Current anchors are strongest for firm concentration, labor-share/wage diffusion, financial-asset ownership, and recent index concentration. ALFRED creates a credible path to PIT macro reconstruction, but exact series-by-series vintage coverage remains a G2 task. Pre-2003 S&P 500 Equal Weight history is explicitly back-tested and may not be used as native T0 evidence.

## 3. D-A Index–Median Divergence

**Desktop prior: STRONG CANDIDATE, CONDITIONAL.** Recent top-10 S&P concentration near 40% makes cap-weight dominance economically important. The correct target is relative breadth/concentration, not absolute index return. Historical equal-weight comparisons before the 2003 live launch are a hard PIT boundary because S&P labels earlier history hypothetical back-tested data.

## 4. D-B GDP–Household Divergence

**Desktop prior: MODERATE / CYCLICAL.** Cross-OECD evidence that includes the US shows productivity–median-wage decoupling, but household diffusion can re-accelerate under tight labor markets or transfers. A static “GDP up / household down” label is too crude.

## 5. D-C Profit–Employment Divergence

**Desktop prior: STRONG.** Superstar-firm evidence is the most direct mechanism anchor: higher concentration, high profits and lower labor share can coexist. R0 still lacks a vintage-safe leader employment-share panel, so D-C is not replay-qualified.

## 6. Candidate regime episodes

- 1990s–2000s: `KR1 PRODUCTIVITY_DIVERGENCE` candidate as frontier firms pull away.
- 2010s–mid-2020s: `KR2 CAPITAL_CONCENTRATION` / `KR3 ASSET_OWNERSHIP_DIVERGENCE` candidate as profit/market concentration interacts with concentrated equity ownership.
- These labels are retrospective desktop candidates only; exact T0 transitions remain untested.

## 7. Asset-transmission evidence

Allowed inference: firm/profit concentration can shift earnings capture toward cap-weight leaders; concentrated equity ownership can amplify household wealth dispersion. Required Price Gate remains `valuation + discount rate + liquidity + market expectations`; therefore K does not imply automatic large-cap outperformance or positive absolute index return.

## 8. Counterevidence and hard negatives

- `HN3 VALUATION_BUBBLE`: concentrated leaders can underperform when starting valuations dominate.
- `HN4 DATA_ARTIFACT`: pre-2003 equal-weight history is back-tested rather than live.
- Labor-market tightening or fiscal transfers can compress D-B without reversing K02/K03.

## 9. PIT readiness by period

- 1990s–2000s macro: `PIT_RECONSTRUCTABLE` candidate via ALFRED, per-series audit required.
- Firm-level concentration/employment: `UNKNOWN/PARTIAL`; historical microdata availability does not equal public T0 availability.
- 2003+ equal-weight market comparisons: materially better live-index provenance, but constituents/corporate actions still require exact replay reconstruction.
- Current DFA and concentration snapshots: `CURRENT_ONLY` in R0.

## 10. Comparability limits

OECD wage/productivity definitions are cross-country usable only within their stated sample/method. DFA wealth ownership is US-native. Market-cap versus equal-weight comparisons are not definitionally identical to median stock return and must not be silently substituted.

## 11. Current scientific status

`STRONG_PRIOR / FORMAL_PIT_VALIDATION_REQUIRED`. US evidence strongly supports H1/H2 and provides strong H3 context. H7 is conditional; H8 absolute-return timing remains unproven.
