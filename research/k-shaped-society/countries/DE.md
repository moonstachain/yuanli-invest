# DE｜1990–2026 K-Shaped Research Dossier

## 1. Scope and data boundary

Germany is primarily a **policy/institutional counterfactual** in R0. Current evidence is strongest for labor-institution transmission and recent large-index versus broad-market performance. It is weaker for firm-level profit concentration, household wealth ownership, opportunity persistence, and regional K. OECD revision infrastructure may support PIT reconstruction of selected macro series, but exact German historical source bundles are not yet qualified.

## 3. D-A Index–Median Divergence

**Desktop prior: MODERATE / CONFOUNDED.** Recent DAX returns exceeded the broader Regulated Market All Share in the displayed STOXX comparison. This cannot be read directly as domestic K because large German listed firms have different sector and international revenue exposure. Treat sector/global-exposure composition as an `HN7` confounder until controlled.

## 4. D-B GDP–Household Divergence

**Desktop prior: MODERATE, POLICY-SENSITIVE.** OECD evidence shows productivity–median wage decoupling across countries including Germany, while bargaining institutions affect the degree of pass-through. Germany is therefore especially useful for testing H6: institutional structure may compress or amplify the slope from production K to household outcomes.

## 5. D-C Profit–Employment Divergence

**Desktop prior: INDETERMINATE.** R0 lacks a Germany-specific, vintage-safe firm profit/employment concentration panel. DAX leadership is not an acceptable substitute.

## 6. Candidate regime episodes

- 1990s–2010s: possible `KR1 PRODUCTIVITY_DIVERGENCE` mixed with institutional compression and bargaining decentralisation.
- Recent years: possible market concentration signal, but `KR2` must remain unassigned until profit/employment evidence exists.

## 7. Asset-transmission evidence

DAX versus broad-market relative performance is admissible only as a **relative-return candidate**. Before attributing it to K, the replay must control sector composition, global revenue exposure, rates and valuation. The correct chain is `structural state -> earnings capture -> valuation/liquidity -> relative return`, not `Germany K -> DAX buy`.

## 8. Counterevidence and hard negatives

- `HN5 POLICY_COMPRESSION`: coordinated wage-setting, vocational institutions, public services and redistribution may weaken downstream household divergence.
- `HN7 SECTOR_ROTATION`: DAX outperformance can arise from industry/global exposure rather than domestic K.
- Weak or stagnant aggregate growth can create apparent dispersion without a true K transition.

## 9. PIT readiness by period

- OECD-covered macro releases: `PIT_RECONSTRUCTABLE` candidate via revisions infrastructure; exact series audit required.
- Bargaining coverage history: `CURRENT_ONLY` retrospective evidence in R0.
- DAX/broad-market recent comparison: `CURRENT_ONLY`; formal historical replay requires exact live index histories and composition.

## 10. Comparability limits

Germany's bargaining institutions make direct labor-outcome comparison with the US or Korea structurally non-neutral. Market-index comparisons across countries also differ by sector composition, free-float rules and international revenue exposure.

## 11. Current scientific status

`FORMAL_PIT_VALIDATION_REQUIRED`. Germany is a particularly valuable H6/HN5/HN7 control case, but R0 evidence is insufficient to call a full K regime.
