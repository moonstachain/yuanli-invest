# YKS0-R0｜Three Divergences × Asset-Transmission Map

**Authority:** `RESEARCH_CANDIDATE_ONLY`  
**Boundary:** relative transmission research; no allocation or execution authority.

## 1. Why the asset layer must be downstream

A K-shaped structural state is not a price signal. The required chain is:

`KRegime -> Value Capture -> Earnings/Cash-flow Distribution -> Ownership -> Price/Valuation/Discount Rate/Liquidity -> Relative Return Candidate`

The Price Gate is not optional. A structurally dominant firm can still produce a poor investment return if valuation already discounts the advantage, discount rates rise, liquidity contracts, or a sector-specific cycle reverses.

## 2. D-A｜Index–Median Divergence

### Structural object

Cap-weight index leadership relative to median/equal-weight/broader-market outcomes.

### Primary K inputs

`K02 FIRM + K03 PROFIT + K06 WEALTH/OWNERSHIP`.

### Candidate mechanism

Frontier-firm productivity and profit concentration raise the share of index earnings and market capitalization controlled by a small group. If those firms continue to capture incremental cash flows, a capitalization-weighted index can outperform broader or median representations.

### Mandatory Price Gate

- starting valuation and relative multiple;
- real/nominal discount rates;
- liquidity/financial conditions;
- sector and global-revenue exposure;
- market expectations and positioning;
- live-index methodology, constituents and corporate actions.

### Current five-country evidence

- **US:** strong concentration candidate, but pre-2003 equal-weight history is back-tested; formal long-run replay must fail closed there.
- **DE:** DAX relative strength is confounded by sector/global exposure and is therefore a candidate `HN7` rather than clean proof.
- **JP:** Large70 underperformance versus TOPIX to Oct-2025 is a direct counterexample to “firm K implies large-cap outperformance.”
- **KR:** South Korea 200 recent outperformance is supportive but highly exposed to semiconductor/sector beta.
- **CN:** R0 has no survivorship-safe A-share constituent/breadth reconstruction; status remains indeterminate.

### R0 conclusion

H7 should predict **relative breadth/concentration probability**, not absolute index direction. Current authority: `CONDITIONAL_RELATIVE_PRIOR`.

## 3. D-B｜GDP–Household Divergence

### Structural object

Aggregate productivity/output grows faster than median wage/income/consumption or employment quality.

### Primary K inputs

`K01 INDUSTRY + K04 EMPLOYMENT + K05 LABOR + K06 WEALTH + K09 POLICY`.

### Candidate mechanism

Aggregate value creation can remain positive while labor intensity falls, bargaining pass-through weakens, gains accrue to capital owners, or policy only partially redistributes the increment. The asset consequence depends on who owns the claims on the winning cash flows and whether household weakness feeds back into demand or policy.

### Asset-transmission candidates

- relative advantage of firms whose demand is global or whose margins are less dependent on median domestic household income;
- possible relative weakness of domestic broad-consumption exposures when household diffusion persistently lags;
- stronger wealth effects where financial-asset ownership is concentrated;
- policy-sensitive reversal when wages, services or transfers re-diffuse gains.

These are mechanisms to test, not investment instructions.

### Current five-country evidence

- **JP:** strongest historical desktop example through BOJ productivity–wage research.
- **CN:** current official 2025 data show productivity growing faster than median income/consumption; this is weaker diffusion, not household contraction.
- **DE:** institutional design is a key transmission modifier.
- **US:** evidence exists but household diffusion is more cyclical and transfer-sensitive.
- **KR:** plausible but R0 median-household evidence is thinner than D-C evidence.

### R0 conclusion

`STRONG_PRIOR / POLICY_SENSITIVE / FORMAL_PIT_VALIDATION_REQUIRED`.

## 4. D-C｜Profit–Employment Divergence

### Structural object

Profit/output/export winners capture more incremental value without proportional employment expansion.

### Primary K inputs

`K02 FIRM + K03 PROFIT + K04 EMPLOYMENT + K05 LABOR`.

### Candidate mechanism

Software, IP, automation, scale, network effects, capital intensity and globalized production allow revenue/profit to compound faster than headcount. Value capture becomes concentrated while labor diffusion weakens.

### Asset-transmission candidates

The most direct candidate is not “buy large caps,” but a shift in relative **earnings quality and cash-flow capture** toward control-point firms. Only after the Price Gate may this become a relative-return hypothesis.

### Current five-country evidence

- **US:** superstar-firm research supplies the clearest mechanism evidence.
- **KR:** strongest Winner-Less-Hiring desktop case: top groups lead exports but account for a very small employment share; SMEs employ most workers.
- **JP:** large-small profit-per-employee gap is strong evidence of firm outcome divergence, though employment share still needs direct reconstruction.
- **CN:** industry profit divergence is strong, but employment diffusion is the decisive missing half of the pair.
- **DE:** insufficient firm-level R0 evidence; useful control rather than support case.

### Kill gate

If D-C cannot materially outperform `B0_AGGREGATE_ONLY` in preregistered PIT replay, YKS0 should be downgraded. It is the theory's most direct structural claim and should not survive on narrative flexibility.

## 5. Relative-return hypotheses by regime

### KR0 BROAD_DIFFUSION

Candidate market signature: improving breadth, median firm outcomes and household diffusion. A cap-weight leadership premium should be less structurally necessary. This is not a claim that small/equal-weight assets must rise.

### KR1 PRODUCTIVITY_DIVERGENCE

Candidate signature: frontier firms separate operationally before downstream household divergence is complete. The investment-relevant variable is whether earnings capture and valuation have followed the productivity gap.

### KR2 CAPITAL_CONCENTRATION

Candidate signature: K02 + K03 widening with weaker K04/K05 diffusion. This is the strongest structural precondition for cap-weight/leader earnings concentration, but still requires Price-Gate validation.

### KR3 ASSET_OWNERSHIP_DIVERGENCE

Candidate signature: strong asset returns plus concentrated participation amplify wealth outcomes. This affects both social diffusion and feedback into spending/financial conditions.

### KR4 OPPORTUNITY_LOCK_IN

Candidate signature: longer persistence, regional/educational inheritance and reduced mobility. R0 has insufficient evidence to map this reliably to assets and therefore leaves the transmission largely UNKNOWN.

### KR5 REDIFFUSION

Candidate signature: K04/K05 and/or K02 gaps narrow even while some profit concentration persists. Potential market implication is broader earnings breadth, but Korea's catch-up evidence must first be converted into PIT state rather than treated as an automatic style call.

## 6. Required controls before G4

Any later K-to-asset benchmark must explicitly control aggregate growth, inflation, rates, credit/liquidity, starting valuation, momentum/market trend, sector composition, foreign-revenue exposure, constituent survivorship and corporate actions. Otherwise apparent K alpha can be conventional factor beta in disguise.

## 7. Scientific boundary

R0 records transmission candidates only. It does not establish asset alpha, portfolio suitability, sizing, or execution authority. H8 remains `ABSOLUTE_RETURN_UNPROVEN / RELATIVE_TRANSMISSION_CANDIDATE` until G4 follows successful G2/G3 evidence and replay gates.
