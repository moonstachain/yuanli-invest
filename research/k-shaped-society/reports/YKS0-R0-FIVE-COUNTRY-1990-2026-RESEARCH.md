# YKS0-R0｜1990–2026 Five-Country K-Shaped Research Synthesis

**Authority:** `RESEARCH_CANDIDATE`  
**Scientific status:** `STRONG_PRIOR / FORMAL_PIT_VALIDATION_REQUIRED`  
**Countries:** US / DE / JP / KR / CN  
**Question:** Can a nine-dimensional distribution-regime model explain, before the fact, three macro divergences better than aggregate macro, market-only, or inequality-only baselines?

## 1. Executive finding

R0 does **not** establish that YKS0 has passed a historical Point-in-Time replay. It establishes something narrower and more useful: the five-country evidence is strong enough, heterogeneous enough, and falsifiable enough to justify a formal PIT program, while also showing that the three target divergences should not be treated as equally easy.

The current desktop ordering is:

`D-C Profit–Employment Divergence > D-B GDP–Household Divergence > D-A Index–Median Divergence`

This is an ordering of **expected structural explanatory power**, not a return forecast and not a scientific settlement. D-C has the most direct cross-country mechanism evidence; D-B has strong wage/productivity and institutional evidence; D-A is conditional on a separate Price Gate containing valuation, rates, liquidity, sector mix and expectations.

The deepest candidate conclusion is therefore not “K-shaped society predicts stocks.” It is:

> **Macro intelligence needs to measure Growth × Diffusion × Capture. GDP measures much of Growth; traditional inequality often observes downstream outcomes; the K Vector is intended to measure the changing diffusion process and who captures the increment.**

## 2. What is source fact versus research inference

### Source facts currently admitted

- OECD research documents productivity–median compensation decoupling across many countries, including Germany, Japan, Korea and the US in its comparative windows.
- Autor et al. document rising concentration in high-profit, lower-labor-share superstar firms and larger labor-share declines where concentration rose most.
- Federal Reserve DFA evidence shows extremely low bottom-half ownership of corporate equities and mutual funds in the current US distribution.
- BOJ research documents Japan's late-1990s onward productivity–real-wage gap and a very large deterioration in small-firm operating profit per employee relative to large firms.
- OECD Korea evidence shows weak employment diffusion from large business groups, a very large SME productivity gap, and—critically—a strong 2013–2024 SME catch-up counter-signal.
- China NBS data show 2025 productivity growth exceeding median-income/consumption growth and very large 2025–2026 industrial-profit dispersion across sectors.
- World Bank WDI China Gini data do not show a monotonic recent increase, directly falsifying any equation of K with a mechanically rising Gini.
- Index-provider evidence supplies recent market-breadth/concentration observations, but its historical use is bounded by launch dates, backtests, constituent histories and methodology changes.

### Research inferences, still below replay authority

- US is the strongest candidate for `production/profit K -> asset ownership amplification`.
- Germany is more valuable as an institutional compression/hard-negative case than as a simple high-K exemplar.
- Japan is a Gold candidate for D-B and a useful D-A falsifier.
- Korea is a Gold candidate for D-C and simultaneously a KR5/rediffusion counterexample because SME productivity recently caught up at the margin.
- China currently shows a plausible `growth-with-weaker-diffusion` transition, but the strongest missing object is still direct profit-versus-employment diffusion at the firm/sector level.

None of these inferences is labeled replay-qualified in R0.

## 3. The three divergence targets

### D-A｜Index–Median Divergence

Question: does a cap-weighted index or large-cap cohort outperform the median/equal-weight/broader market while leadership breadth narrows?

**Why it is hardest:** price is not a direct projection of Reality. Even if K02/K03 identify true profit concentration, relative returns still depend on starting valuation, discount rates, liquidity, sector exposure and expectations. The same structural K can therefore produce different asset outcomes.

**Evidence pattern:** US and Korea provide recent support candidates; Germany provides an important sector/global-exposure confounder; Japan provides a direct recent counterexample where Large70 underperformed TOPIX.

**PIT hazard:** the S&P 500 Equal Weight Index launched in 2003; pre-launch history is explicitly hypothetical back-tested data. A model using that history as if investors observed a live equal-weight index would commit hindsight leakage.

**R0 status:** `CONDITIONAL_RELATIVE_PRIOR`.

### D-B｜GDP–Household Divergence

Question: can output/productivity remain resilient while median real wage, median disposable income, labor share, consumption or employment quality diffuse more slowly?

**Evidence pattern:** OECD cross-country research supports productivity–median-pay decoupling. Japan supplies a particularly strong long-run mechanism case. China 2025 supplies a current official example in which labor productivity grew 6.1% while median household income and consumption grew more slowly. Germany highlights that institutions can change the slope of transmission.

**Critical semantic boundary:** weaker diffusion does not mean household income is falling. For China 2025, the admissible inference is that incremental productivity diffused more slowly to median households, not that residents experienced no income growth.

**R0 status:** `STRONG_PRIOR / POLICY_SENSITIVE`.

### D-C｜Profit–Employment Divergence

Question: can frontier/large firms capture a rising share of profit, exports or productivity without proportional employment expansion?

**Evidence pattern:** US superstar-firm evidence gives the mechanism; Korea is particularly strong because the top business groups led export activity while accounting for a tiny share of total employment; Japan shows a major large-small profit-per-worker gap. China displays very large current industry-profit dispersion but still lacks the employment side of the pair.

**Key kill gate:** if formal PIT replay cannot beat aggregate baselines on D-C, the K model should be materially downgraded. This is the most direct structural claim and therefore should be the hardest internal test of whether YKS0 adds value.

**R0 status:** `STRONGEST_PRIOR / FORMAL_PIT_VALIDATION_REQUIRED`.

## 5. Nine-dimensional synthesis

### K01 INDUSTRY

Current China and Korean evidence supports the importance of sector composition, but long-run comparable sector transition evidence is incomplete. K01 should not be inferred from “tech is strong”; it needs value-added/capital-intensity/service/IP measures with stable definitions.

### K02 FIRM

Strongest in US/Japan/Korea. Korea demonstrates why both Level and Δ matter: the large-SME productivity gap remains high, yet SME productivity grew much faster from 2013 to 2024. A level-only model would miss rediffusion.

### K03 PROFIT

Strong in US mechanism evidence and recent China/Korea/Japan observations, but measurement objects differ: market-cap concentration, profit-per-worker, sector profit dispersion and top-decile profit share are not interchangeable.

### K04 EMPLOYMENT

This is the decisive missing dimension in several countries. Korea supplies the clearest structural evidence. China needs the largest remediation: current profit divergence cannot be promoted into Winner-Less-Hiring without employment diffusion data.

### K05 LABOR

Cross-country OECD and BOJ evidence makes K05 one of the best evidenced dimensions. It is also highly endogenous to institutions and cycle; therefore country comparability requires careful definition and K09 context.

### K06 WEALTH

US has strong current ownership evidence. Other countries remain materially under-sourced. Income Gini cannot substitute for asset participation/ownership; the China WDI series is therefore counterevidence against conceptual collapse, not a K06 measurement solution.

### K07 REGION / K08 OPPORTUNITY

These are the weakest R0 evidence areas across all five countries. Their weakness is a program feature, not a documentation problem: G2 must decide whether authoritative, PIT-reconstructable evidence exists before any full-state claim.

### K09 POLICY

Germany and Japan show why policy/institutions are endogenous. Korea's SME catch-up is an important empirical counter-signal, but R0 cannot causally attribute that catch-up to specific policy without separate identification.

## 7. Hard negatives already admitted

R0 explicitly preserves cases capable of defeating the preferred story:

- `HN2 AGGREGATE_STAGNATION`: Japan post-bubble weakness and Germany 2024–26 candidate weak-growth control.
- `HN3 VALUATION_BUBBLE`: US dot-com concentration may be valuation rather than structural K.
- `HN4 DATA_ARTIFACT`: back-tested index histories, revised macro series and classification changes.
- `HN5 POLICY_COMPRESSION`: Germany/pandemic cases can show high production K but compressed household K.
- `HN6 ASSET_LEVEL_DROP`: falling asset prices do not prove ownership convergence.
- `HN7 SECTOR_ROTATION`: Germany and Korea can exhibit large-index leadership because of sector/global beta.
- `HN8 CATCHUP/REDIFFUSION`: Korea 2013–2024 SME catch-up directly challenges monotonic K.

A valid YKS0 engine must reject these false positives rather than explain them away after reveal.

## 8. Evidence-sufficiency reality

The largest risk to this research program is not model choice. It is **historical evidence sufficiency**. Market prices are comparatively easy. The difficult layers are historical constituents, firm accounting vintages, release calendars, revised macro data, archived China series, changing definitions, and the ability to prove what was actually available at T0.

Current strongest PIT infrastructure anchors are ALFRED for US real-time periods and OECD Short-Term Economic Statistics Revisions for published macro snapshots. They do not automatically solve all firm, household, wealth, regional or China evidence.

Therefore R0 does not treat “authoritative retrospective history” as equivalent to PIT eligibility.

## 9. What the formal replay must test

For each historical case, freeze T0 and visible evidence before reveal, construct the nine-dimensional vector with explicit UNKNOWNs, output regime candidate / binding dimensions / 3Y and 5Y transitions / transmission candidates / falsifiers, then reveal future Reality. Compare against:

- `B0_AGGREGATE_ONLY`
- `B1_MARKET_ONLY`
- `B2_INEQUALITY_ONLY`

The K model earns scientific authority only if it adds out-of-sample discrimination, rejects hard negatives, handles near twins, and remains calibrated under missing evidence.

## 10. Research conclusion

The desktop evidence supports a shift in macro framing from a one-dimensional question—“how much did the economy grow?”—toward a three-part structure:

`Growth × Diffusion × Capture`.

YKS0's candidate contribution is the **Diffusion/Distribution Regime layer**. It may explain why aggregate growth, typical household outcomes, corporate profit concentration, employment diffusion and asset ownership diverge from one another. But its investment value is not assumed. It must first survive five-country PIT reconstruction and blind replay.

**Settlement:** `STRONG_PRIOR / FORMAL_PIT_VALIDATION_REQUIRED`.
