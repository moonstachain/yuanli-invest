## Human Review dimensions

For each candidate, review seven dimensions independently:

1. **Theory fit** — are the cited theories genuine ancestors of the candidate mechanism rather than adjacent famous papers?
2. **Mechanism fidelity** — has QXM2 preserved what the primary source actually claims without strengthening it?
3. **Identification strength** — are theory, association, prediction, and causality kept distinct?
4. **Contradictory / competing evidence** — has the pack preserved evidence that limits the preferred story?
5. **Replication / external validity** — are market, era, sector, accounting-regime and sample-transfer limits explicit?
6. **Operationalization / PIT testability** — can the proposed hypothesis actually be tested with point-in-time observables and a real falsifier?
7. **Admission recommendation** — should the object later advance, advance only with a boundary, remain interpretation-only, stay shadow, or be rejected/revised?

Permitted recommendation values:

`advance` · `advance_with_boundary` · `interpretation_only` · `keep_shadow` · `reject_or_revise`

## Candidate 01｜Fundamental Driver Decomposition

Evidence spine: Lev–Thiagarajan (1993), Abarbanell–Bushee (1997), Nissim–Penman (2001).

Mechanism thesis: economically meaningful decomposition can provide information beyond headline metrics, while the hierarchy itself is not causal proof and cannot be frozen as a universal cross-sector checklist.

Competing/boundary issue: signal definitions, sector structure, accounting regimes and macro states can materially alter usefulness.

Operationalization: `HYP-P-201-DRIVER-INCREMENTAL-OOS`, `HYP-P-202-DRIVER-REGIME-STABILITY`; Benchmark Seed `QXM2-BSEED-P003-DRIVER-OOS`.

Provisional recommendation for Human Review: `advance_with_boundary`.

Human question: Is the proposed driver ontology primitive and stable enough to justify later Registry admission, while preserving sector-specific profiles and requiring OOS incremental information versus simpler revenue/margin/consensus baselines?

## Candidate 02｜Three-Statement Integrity & Cash Conversion

Evidence spine: Dechow (1994), Sloan (1996), Dechow–Dichev (2002), Richardson–Sloan–Soliman–Tuna (2005), Fairfield–Whisenant–Yohn (2003), plus IAS 7 as normative reporting authority only.

Mechanism thesis: earnings, accruals, cash flow and balance-sheet stocks carry different information; reliability and cash conversion matter, but timing/matching and normal investment/growth can explain divergence.

Competing/boundary issue: `cash flow > earnings` is not a valid universal rule; divergence is diagnostic and cannot become a fraud inference.

Operationalization: `HYP-P-203-CASH-CONVERSION-PERSISTENCE`, `HYP-P-204-ACCRUAL-RELIABILITY`; Benchmark Seed `QXM2-BSEED-P004-CASH-CONVERSION`.

Provisional recommendation for Human Review: `advance_with_boundary`.

Human question: Are the growth/investment, timing/matching and accounting-regime boundaries strong enough to keep the future capability from becoming a false-alarm or misconduct-scoring engine?

## Candidate 03｜Credit & Balance-Sheet Transmission

Evidence spine: Bernanke–Gertler (1989), Kiyotaki–Moore (1997), Schularick–Taylor (2012), Jordà–Schularick–Taylor (2013).

Mechanism thesis: borrower net worth, collateral, lending/funding conditions and credit creation can amplify real and financial fluctuations.

Competing/boundary issue: aggregate credit correlation alone is insufficient evidence of the proposed causal channel; borrower sector and balance-sheet transmission must remain observable.

Operationalization: `HYP-P-205-CREDIT-SECTORAL-TRANSMISSION`, `HYP-P-206-COLLATERAL-FEEDBACK`; Benchmark Seed `QXM2-BSEED-R01-CREDIT-TRANSMISSION`.

Provisional recommendation for Human Review: `advance_with_boundary`.

Human question: Are the proposed sectoral PIT observables sufficient to distinguish net-worth/collateral transmission from generic macro correlation, and is causal language appropriately fail-closed when those intermediates are missing?

## Candidate 04｜Opportunity-Cost / Discount-Rate Bridge

Evidence spine: Sharpe (1964), Campbell–Shiller (1988), Cochrane (2011), Welch–Goyal (2008).

Mechanism thesis: prices can be interpreted through expected cash-flow and discount-rate components under explicit assumptions, but prediction is a separate and much harder claim.

Competing/boundary issue: OOS predictor instability strongly limits any transition from decomposition to timing authority.

Operationalization: `HYP-V-201-EXPECTATION-DECOMPOSITION`, `HYP-V-202-OOS-DISCOUNT-RATE`; Benchmark Seed `QXM2-BSEED-V01-EXPECTATION-DECOMPOSITION`.

Provisional recommendation for Human Review: `interpretation_only`.

Human question: Should this object remain an expectations-decomposition capability unless and until a later preregistered OOS benchmark demonstrates stable predictive value over simple baselines?

## Candidate 05｜Stress Exit Liquidity

Evidence spine: Kyle (1985), Amihud (2002), Pástor–Stambaugh (2003), Brunnermeier–Pedersen (2009).

Mechanism thesis: transaction liquidity, market liquidity, systematic liquidity risk and funding liquidity are distinct; stress exit depends on more than normal-state turnover or spread.

Competing/boundary issue: ADV or a narrow spread can look benign while depth, funding constraints, margin/haircut or cross-market stress deteriorate.

Operationalization: `HYP-S-201-STRESS-LIQUIDITY-INCREMENTAL`, `HYP-S-202-FUNDING-LIQUIDITY-SPIRAL`; Benchmark Seed `QXM2-BSEED-S004-STRESS-EXIT`.

Provisional recommendation for Human Review: `advance_with_boundary`.

Human question: Are funding-liquidity and stress-state observables sufficiently PIT and practically measurable across the intended asset universes to justify later formal benchmark design?

## Candidate 06｜Return Source Attribution

Evidence spine: Brinson–Hood–Beebower (1986), Ibbotson–Kaplan (2000), Campbell (1991), Fama–French (1993).

Mechanism thesis: realized return can be decomposed under explicit portfolio, news-decomposition or factor models, but accounting identity and statistical explanation do not establish causality.

Competing/boundary issue: benchmark choice and model choice change attribution; realized P&L must never rewrite the immutable original ResearchReceipt.

Operationalization: `HYP-CROSS-201-RETURN-IDENTITY-RECONSTRUCTION`, `HYP-CROSS-202-THESIS-FIDELITY`; Benchmark Seed `QXM2-BSEED-CROSS001-RETURN-ATTRIBUTION`.

Provisional recommendation for Human Review: `advance_with_boundary`.

Human question: Does the router preserve asset-form-specific attribution, residual uncertainty and thesis fidelity strongly enough to prevent hindsight explanation from masquerading as research learning?
